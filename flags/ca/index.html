Flag of Canada

Canada is the second-largest country in the world by total area, spanning six time zones from the Atlantic to the Pacific to the Arctic Ocean. It is a bilingual nation with English and French as official languages, reflecting its complex colonial history and the distinct heritage of its Francophone population, particularly in Quebec. Canada is known for its vast and diverse natural landscapes, multicultural cities, and a universal healthcare system.

What the flag of Canada means

Adopted in 1965 after a long national debate, the Maple Leaf flag replaced the Canadian Red Ensign. Red and white were proclaimed Canada's official colours by King George V in 1921, and the maple leaf had been a Canadian emblem since the 19th century. The stylised leaf has eleven points. National Flag of Canada Day is 15 February.

How to recognise it

Vertical red-white-red with a red maple leaf in the white centre.

Did you know?

Canada has the world's longest coastline at over 202,000 km and 20% of the world's freshwater.

Flag history of Canada

Every flag that has flown over Canada, from today back through time.

1965–present

The Maple Leaf Flag

Adopted February 15, 1965 after a passionate national debate. The single red maple leaf on a white field was chosen over dozens of competing designs after months of parliamentary argument known as the "Great Canadian Flag Debate."

1957–1965

Canadian Red Ensign (1957–1965)

A Red Ensign defaced with the updated 1957 Canadian coat of arms. This flag was flown on federal buildings and represented Canada internationally in the years immediately before the Maple Leaf was adopted.

1921–1957

Canadian Red Ensign (1921–1957)

The Red Ensign bearing the 1921 Canadian coat of arms, which added the fleurs-de-lis of France and the English lions. This version was used after Canada formally received its own coat of arms from the Crown.

1868–1921

Canadian Red Ensign (1868–1921)

The earliest Canadian Red Ensign, defaced with the shield of the original four provinces at Confederation: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. Used informally as Canada expanded westward.

1837–1838

Patriote Movement (Lower Canada)

During the 1837–38 rebellions, French-Canadian Patriotes in Lower Canada (Quebec) flew a green-white-red horizontal tricolor against British colonial rule. The uprising was crushed, but the flag endures as a Québécois nationalist symbol.

1534–1763

New France

For over two centuries before British rule, the St Lawrence valley and a vast arc of the continent from Acadia to Louisiana were New France — the French colonial empire of fur traders, missionaries and habitants, flying the royal banner of the French crown (gold fleurs-de-lis on blue). France ceded it to Britain in 1763 after the Seven Years' War, but its language, faith and place names endure across Quebec and beyond.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Canada.

1869–1949

Dominion of Newfoundland

The unofficial 'Pink, White and Green' originated with St. John's fishermen's societies. Newfoundland was a self-governing dominion until it joined Canada in 1949.

1837–1838

Republic of Canada (1837)

Blue-white-blue with two white stars for Upper and Lower Canada, flown by William Lyon Mackenzie's provisional government during the Upper Canada Rebellion.

1827

Republic of Madawaska

A white field with an eagle and a ring of red stars, emblem of the self-declared 'republic' in the disputed Maine–New Brunswick borderland (the design itself is a later commemorative one).

1837–1838

Patriote Movement (Lower Canada)

The green-white-red flag of the Patriotes who rose against British rule in the 1837–38 Lower Canada Rebellion; it remains a symbol of Quebec nationalism today.
