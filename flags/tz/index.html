Flag of Tanzania

Tanzania is East Africa's largest country and home to some of the continent's most iconic landscapes and wildlife. Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest peak, the Serengeti's annual wildebeest migration, and the Ngorongoro Crater are all within its borders. The semi-autonomous archipelago of Zanzibar, once a powerful independent sultanate and centre of the Indian Ocean spice trade, merged with mainland Tanganyika to form Tanzania in 1964.

What the flag of Tanzania means

Green represents the land and its vegetation, yellow the mineral wealth, black the people, and blue the many lakes and rivers and the Indian Ocean. The diagonal design combines the flags of Tanganyika and Zanzibar, which united in 1964 to form Tanzania.

More about the flag

Tanganyika became independent from Britain in 1961 with a green flag crossed by a black band edged in gold. Zanzibar's revolutionary flag of 1964 was blue, black and green. When the two united on 26 April 1964, the new flag combined them diagonally. Tanzania is home to Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest mountain, and to the Serengeti.

How to recognise it

Diagonal black stripe with yellow borders going from lower left to upper right; green top left, blue bottom right.

Did you know?

Tanzania is home to Mount Kilimanjaro, the highest peak in Africa at 5,895 metres.

Flag history of Tanzania

Every flag that has flown over Tanzania, from today back through time.

1964–present

Flag of Tanzania

After Tanganyika and Zanzibar united in 1964, a new flag merged Tanganyika's green and black with Zanzibar's blue into a yellow-edged black diagonal. Green is land, blue sea, black the people, yellow mineral wealth.

1961–1964

Flag of Tanganyika

Independent Tanganyika flew a green field with a central black, yellow-edged stripe — colors that carried directly into the union flag.

Zanzibar, which united with Tanganyika to form Tanzania:

1963–1964

Sultanate of Zanzibar

Zanzibar gained independence from Britain as a constitutional sultanate in December 1963, flying a plain red flag (the historic colour of its Omani-descended dynasty). It lasted barely a month before being overthrown.

1964

People's Republic of Zanzibar

The January 1964 revolution overthrew the sultan and raised a blue-black-green flag. In April 1964 it merged with Tanganyika to form Tanzania — the "Tan" + "Zan" of the new name.

975–1513

Kilwa Sultanate

A fabulously wealthy Swahili-coast sultanate on an island off Tanzania, controlling the medieval gold trade from Great Zimbabwe.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Tanzania.

1856–1964

Sultanate of Zanzibar

The plain red banner derives from the Sultanate of Oman, whose Busaidi dynasty ruled Zanzibar until the 1964 revolution that led to union with Tanganyika as Tanzania.

1961–1964

Tanganyika

Newly independent Tanganyika flew a green-black-green flag before merging with Zanzibar in 1964; the union's name, Tanzania, fused 'Tan-' from Tanganyika with 'Zan-' from Zanzibar.

Neighbouring countries

Tanzania shares a land border with 8 countries: Burundi, the DR Congo, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Rwanda, Uganda and Zambia.
