Flag of Portugal

Portugal occupies the southwestern tip of continental Europe and was once the centre of one of history's most far-reaching maritime empires. Portuguese navigators in the 15th and 16th centuries opened sea routes to Africa, Asia, and the Americas, fundamentally reshaping global trade. Today Portugal is known for its fado music, azulejo tilework, pastel de nata custard tarts, port wine, and is one of the oldest nation-states in Europe.

What the flag of Portugal means

Green and red were adopted after the 1910 revolution that ended the monarchy; green is linked to hope and red to the blood of those who died for the nation. The yellow armillary sphere recalls the navigators of the Age of Discovery, and the shield holds seven castles and five blue shields, symbols of the Portuguese kings since the 12th century. The flag was officially adopted in 1911.

How to recognise it

Vertical green-red with a complex coat of arms straddling the two colour sections.

Did you know?

Portugal is the oldest country in Europe with its modern borders, established in 1139.

Flag history of Portugal

Every flag that has flown over Portugal, from today back through time.

1911–present

Republican flag

Adopted in 1911 after the Republic was proclaimed, replacing royal blue-and-white with green and red. The arms sit on an armillary sphere recalling the Age of Discoveries, placed over the boundary of the two colors.

1830–1910

Constitutional Monarchy

A field split blue at the hoist and white at the fly, with the crowned royal arms over an armillary sphere on the dividing line. It flew until the revolution of 5 October 1910 overthrew the monarchy.

1707–1816

White flag with royal arms

A plain white field bearing the Portuguese royal coat of arms, used through the reigns of John V, Joseph I and Maria I. The white field carried no meaning of its own until colors were added in the 19th century.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Portugal.

1139–1910

Kingdom of Portugal

The white-and-blue royal banner with the shield of five 'quinas' flew over the seafaring kingdom that launched the Age of Discovery, until the 1910 republican revolution.

1580–1640

Iberian Union

For sixty years the Spanish and Portuguese crowns were joined under one Habsburg king, briefly creating a single empire spanning the globe, until Portugal revolted in 1640.
