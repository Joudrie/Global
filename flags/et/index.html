Flag of Ethiopia

Ethiopia is the oldest independent country in Africa and one of the world's oldest nations, having successfully resisted European colonisation by defeating Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896. It is the second most populous country in Africa and the most populous landlocked country in the world. Ethiopia is widely considered the birthplace of coffee and is home to some of humanity's earliest known fossil remains, including the famous 'Lucy' skeleton discovered in 1974.

What the flag of Ethiopia means

Green, yellow and red have been Ethiopia's colours since the late 19th century. Because Ethiopia resisted colonisation, they became the model for the Pan-African colours used by many countries at independence. The blue disc with a yellow star, added in 1996, represents the unity and equality of the nations and peoples of Ethiopia.

How to recognise it

Horizontal green-yellow-red with a blue disc and yellow star in the centre.

Did you know?

Ethiopia is the only African country never to be colonised — it defeated Italy at the Battle of Adwa in 1896.

Flag history of Ethiopia

Every flag that has flown over Ethiopia, from today back through time.

2009–present

Flag of Ethiopia

A 2009 standardization fixed the current national emblem — a gold rayed pentagram on a blue disc — whose rays symbolize the equality and unity of Ethiopia's peoples. The red-yellow-green is one of the world's oldest tricolors.

1996–2009

National emblem flag (light disc)

The current design debuted in 1996, adding the gold pentagram on a blue disc; the shade and proportions were tweaked over the following years.

1987–1991

PDRE socialist flag

When Mengistu's People's Democratic Republic was proclaimed in 1987, a state emblem with a red star, gear, and rising sun was added.

1975–1987

Plain Derg tricolour

From 1975 the military Derg flew the bare red-yellow-green tricolour with no emblem, stripping away all imperial symbolism.

1897–1974

Imperial Lion of Judah

Menelik II fixed the tricolour in 1897; from 1914 it bore the crowned Lion of Judah holding a cross-tipped staff, emblem of the Solomonic dynasty — interrupted only by the Italian occupation of 1936–1941.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Ethiopia.

1897–1974

Ethiopian Empire

The crowned Lion of Judah symbolized the Solomonic dynasty's claimed descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. Its green-yellow-red became the model for Pan-African colors.

1647–1887

Emirate of Harar

An independent walled city-state in eastern Ethiopia, a revered centre of Islamic learning, until Emperor Menelik II conquered it in 1887. Its flag bears crossed swords.
