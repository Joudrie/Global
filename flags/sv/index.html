Flag of El Salvador

El Salvador is the smallest and most densely populated country in Central America and the only one without a Caribbean coastline. It is known for its surf culture along the Pacific coast, the ancient Maya city of Joya de Cerén (preserved by volcanic ash and called the 'Pompeii of the Americas'), and its chain of active volcanoes. El Salvador became the first country in the world to adopt Bitcoin as legal tender in 2021.

What the flag of El Salvador means

The blue and white stripes come from the flag of the Federal Republic of Central America (1823–1841); the blue stands for the oceans that surrounded the federation. The coat of arms shows a triangle with five volcanoes for the former member states, a red cap of liberty and a rainbow, with the motto “Dios, Unión, Libertad”. The flag was adopted in 1912.

How to recognise it

Horizontal blue-white-blue with the coat of arms in the white centre stripe.

Flag history of El Salvador

Every flag that has flown over El Salvador, from today back through time.

1912–present

Republic of El Salvador (current)

El Salvador's blue-white-blue horizontal tricolor and its coat of arms (five volcanoes between two oceans, a rainbow, and the motto "God, Union, Liberty") were standardised in 1912. Like all Central American nations, the design descends from the Federal Republic flag. The blue represents the Pacific and Atlantic; the white represents peace. El Salvador is the smallest and most densely populated country in Central America.

1875–1912

Stars-and-stripes era

For nearly four decades El Salvador flew a flag modelled directly on the United States — alternating blue and white horizontal stripes with a red canton bearing white stars. It was a striking departure from the Central American blue-white-blue, and was abandoned in 1912 when the country returned to the federation-style tricolor it still uses today.

1823–1839

Federal Republic of Central America

El Salvador was part of the five-nation Federal Republic of Central America after independence from Spain. The federation's flag became the shared ancestor of all five Central American states' flags. El Salvador in particular saw intense conflict between Liberals and Conservatives throughout the 19th century, with the flag's exact form changing repeatedly before the 1912 standardisation.

1823–1841

Federal Republic of Central America

Its blue-white-blue stripes survive almost unchanged in the flags of Honduras, Nicaragua, El Salvador, and Guatemala. White stood for the land between the two oceans.

1823–1824

United Provinces of Central America

The first union of the Central American provinces after independence from Mexico, flying the blue-white-blue bands that preceded the Federal Republic and survive in five modern flags.

1896–1898

Greater Republic of Central America

A short-lived attempt to reunify Nicaragua, Honduras, and El Salvador; its blue-white-blue flag carried five volcanoes and stars before the union dissolved within two years.
