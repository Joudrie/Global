Flag of Austria

Austria is a landlocked country in Central Europe known for its alpine scenery, imperial architecture, and extraordinary musical heritage. For centuries it was the centre of the Habsburg Empire, one of Europe's most powerful dynasties, which shaped the culture of the entire continent. Vienna, the capital, was home to Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Brahms, and Mahler, and remains one of the world's preeminent cultural cities.

What the flag of Austria means

Legend says Duke Leopold V's white tunic was soaked in blood at the Siege of Acre in 1191 except under his belt, leaving a red-white-red pattern. In fact the colours are the arms of the Babenberg dukes, recorded from the 13th century, which makes this one of the oldest national flag designs still in use. The state flag adds the federal eagle holding a sickle and hammer, with the broken chains added in 1945 to mark liberation.

How to recognise it

Simple horizontal red-white-red stripes — one of the oldest national flags still in use.

Did you know?

Austria is the birthplace of Mozart, Beethoven lived there, and it gave the world the croissant.

Flag history of Austria

Every flag that has flown over Austria, from today back through time.

1945–present

Austria (Second Republic)

With independence restored in 1945, Austria reinstated the red-white-red triband. Legend ties it to a medieval Babenberg duke whose white tunic was left red but for the band under his belt — one of the oldest flag designs still in use.

1938–1945

Anschluss (annexed by Nazi Germany)

Following the Anschluss of March 1938, Austria ceased to exist as an independent state and the Nazi German flag flew over its territory until liberation in 1945.

1934–1938

Federal State (Ständestaat)

The authoritarian Austrofascist Federal State kept the red-white-red bands but added a state emblem with a double-headed eagle and the crutch-cross. It ended with the Nazi annexation.

1918–1934

First Austrian Republic

After the empire collapsed, the new republic adopted the red-white-red triband that is essentially the modern Austrian flag.

1869–1918

Austria-Hungary

The Dual Monarchy used a combined civil ensign joining the Austrian red-white-red with the Hungarian colors, while the black-gold Habsburg flag remained in dynastic use until 1918.

1804–1867

Austrian Empire (Habsburg)

The black-gold horizontal bicolour was the dynastic flag of the House of Habsburg, derived from their long reign as Holy Roman Emperors.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Austria.

1180–1918

Duchy of Styria

The white-over-green Landesfarben of the Habsburg duchy; its arms bear a white fire-breathing panther. Styria is now split between Austria and Slovenia.

976–1918

Duchy of Carinthia

The yellow-red-white crown-land flag of one of the oldest duchies of the Holy Roman Empire, whose arms show three black lions on gold.
