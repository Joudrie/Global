Flag of the Dominican Republic

The Dominican Republic shares the island of Hispaniola with Haiti and was the site of the first permanent European settlement in the Americas, established by Bartholomew Columbus in 1496. It is the most visited country in the Caribbean, with large resort areas along its northern and eastern coasts. Santo Domingo is home to the Cathedral of Santa María la Menor (1512), the oldest cathedral in the Americas.

What the flag of the Dominican Republic means

The white cross stands for the people's struggle to keep their nation and faith, blue for liberty and red for the blood of the independence war. The coat of arms shows an open Bible, making it the only national flag with a Bible, and the motto “Dios, Patria, Libertad”. The flag was designed by Juan Pablo Duarte and adopted in 1844 after independence from Haiti.

How to recognise it

Quartered blue-red with a white cross and coat of arms in the centre.

Flag history of the Dominican Republic

Every flag that has flown over the Dominican Republic, from today back through time.

1844–present

Dominican Republic

The Dominican flag — a white cross dividing blue and red quarters, with the coat of arms at centre — was designed by Juan Pablo Duarte for independence from Haiti in 1844. The white cross was deliberately chosen to set the Dominican flag apart from Haiti's blue-and-red (which had no cross), marking the cultural, religious, and political distinction the founders wanted to emphasise. The coat of arms features a Bible and cross, six Dominican flags, and the motto "God, Fatherland, Liberty."

1822–1844

Haitian Occupation

From 1822 to 1844 the eastern two-thirds of Hispaniola (the future Dominican Republic) was occupied and incorporated into Haiti by President Jean-Pierre Boyer. For 22 years the Haitian blue-and-red flew over what is now the Dominican Republic, a period known in Dominican history as the Haitian Unification — an occupation that profoundly shaped Dominican national identity and the desire for separation.

1821–1822

Republic of Spanish Haiti

For nine weeks in late 1821, the eastern half of Hispaniola declared independence from Spain as the "Republic of Spanish Haiti," flying a Gran Colombia-style tricolor with a white hoist bar. Haiti invaded and annexed it in February 1822, beginning the 22-year occupation.

Republic of Spanish Haiti

A yellow-blue-red tricolor with silver stars, flown by the short-lived breakaway state of Santo Domingo before Haiti annexed it in 1822.

First Dominican Republic

The flag raised when the Dominican Republic won independence from Haiti in 1844, a white cross dividing blue and red quarters — the design, with arms added, that endures today.
