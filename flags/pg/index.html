Flag of Papua New Guinea

Papua New Guinea occupies the eastern half of the island of New Guinea and is one of the world's most linguistically diverse countries, with over 800 languages spoken by a population of around 9 million — more languages than any other country on Earth. The country has extraordinary biodiversity with large areas of intact tropical rainforest and species found nowhere else. Papua New Guinea achieved independence from Australian administration in 1975.

What the flag of Papua New Guinea means

Red and black are traditional colours in the art of many Papua New Guinean peoples. The bird of paradise represents the country's emergence into nationhood, and the Southern Cross its place in the South Pacific. The flag was designed by Susan Karike, a 15-year-old schoolgirl, in 1971.

How to recognise it

Diagonal red-over-black with a yellow bird of paradise on the red and a white Southern Cross on the black.

Flag history of Papua New Guinea

Every flag that has flown over Papua New Guinea, from today back through time.

1971–present

Flag of Papua New Guinea

Designed by 15-year-old schoolgirl Susan Karike and adopted in 1971, it shows a yellow Raggiana bird of paradise and the white stars of the Southern Cross on a diagonally split red-and-black field. It has never changed.

1970–1971

Territory tricolour

A blue-white-green vertical design with stars and a bird of paradise, used briefly for the Territory of Papua and New Guinea before being rejected as too "colonial".

1906–1949

Australian Territory of Papua

Australia took over the southern half — the Territory of Papua — in 1906, flying a British blue ensign defaced with a bird-of-paradise badge, while the German-then-Australian north was administered separately. The two halves were merged into a single Territory of Papua and New Guinea in 1949 (thereafter under the Australian flag) on the road to the unified independence of 1975.
