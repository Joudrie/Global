Flag of Vatican City

Vatican City is the world's smallest internationally recognised country, an independent city-state entirely within Rome covering just 44 hectares. It serves as the headquarters of the Roman Catholic Church and the residence of the Pope, giving this tiny territory immense religious significance for over 1.3 billion Catholics worldwide. Vatican City is home to St Peter's Basilica, the Sistine Chapel with Michelangelo's famous ceiling fresco, and some of the world's most important art and historical collections.

What the flag of Vatican City means

Yellow and white recall the gold and silver keys of Saint Peter, the “keys of the kingdom of heaven” that the Gospel says Christ gave him. The crossed keys are tied with a red cord and topped by the papal tiara. The flag was adopted in 1929, when the Lateran Treaty created Vatican City, and it is one of only two square national flags, with Switzerland's.

More about the flag

The colours of Vatican City continue those of the Papal States, whose ships flew yellow and white from the early 19th century. On the coat of arms, the gold key on the right stands for the power of the papacy in heaven and the silver key on the left for its spiritual authority on earth. At about 0.44 square kilometres, Vatican City is the smallest independent state in the world.

How to recognise it

Vertical yellow-white with the papal tiara and crossed keys emblem on the white half.

Flag history of Vatican City

Every flag that has flown over Vatican City, from today back through time.

2023–present

Yellow-white with keys and tiara

The 2023 Fundamental Law accompanied a minor revision of the emblem. The flag remains one of only two square national flags in the world, alongside Switzerland.

1929–2023

Original Vatican flag (1929)

Adopted with the 1929 Lateran Treaty that created Vatican City, the square yellow-and-white bicolor bears the crossed keys of St. Peter and the papal tiara, derived from the flag of the Papal States.

1808–1870

Papal States (yellow-white)

For over a thousand years the popes ruled a sovereign state across central Italy. In 1808 its flag changed from yellow-and-red to the gold-and-white that still flies over the Vatican — the gold and silver of the keys of Saint Peter. The Papal States were swallowed by a unifying Italy in 1870, leaving the popes stateless until the 1929 Lateran Treaty created tiny Vatican City.

1750–1808

Papal States (yellow-red)

Before 1808 the Papal States flew yellow-and-red — the traditional colours of the senate and people of Rome, inherited from antiquity. Napoleon's occupation prompted the switch to the gold-and-white that became the modern papal colours.
