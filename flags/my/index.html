Flag of Malaysia

Malaysia is a Southeast Asian country consisting of two distinct regions separated by the South China Sea — Peninsular Malaysia (bordering Thailand) and Malaysian Borneo. It is a multicultural melting pot of Malay, Chinese, Indian, and indigenous cultures, reflected in its extraordinarily diverse food, festivals, and languages. The Petronas Twin Towers in Kuala Lumpur were the world's tallest buildings from 1998 to 2004 and remain iconic symbols of Malaysia's rapid development.

What the flag of Malaysia means

The fourteen stripes, the Jalur Gemilang or “Stripes of Glory”, stand for the equal status of the thirteen states and the federal government, as do the fourteen points of the star. The crescent represents Islam, yellow the Malay rulers, and the blue canton the unity of the people. The flag first had eleven stripes in 1950; three were added in 1963 when Sabah, Sarawak and Singapore joined.

How to recognise it

Fourteen alternating red-white stripes with a blue canton containing a crescent and 14-pointed star.

Did you know?

Malaysia is the world's third-largest exporter of rubber and has the world's oldest rainforest.

Flag history of Malaysia

Every flag that has flown over Malaysia, from today back through time.

1963–present

Jalur Gemilang

When Sabah, Sarawak, and Singapore joined in 1963, the stripes and star points increased from 11 to 14. The design was unchanged when Singapore left in 1965 — the 14th element was simply reinterpreted as the federal territories.

1950–1963

Federation of Malaya

Designed by Mohamad Hamzah and approved in 1950, it had 11 red-and-white stripes and an 11-pointed star for the 11 members of the Federation of Malaya.

1470–1623

Pahang Kingdom

The old Pahang Sultanate of the Malay Peninsula, a vassal of Malacca and Johor, ancestor of the modern Malaysian state of Pahang.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Malaysia.

1870–1946

Kingdom of Sarawak

Sarawak was privately ruled by the Brooke family, English 'White Rajahs', for over a century. The split black-and-red cross is from the Brooke coat of arms.

1400–1511

Sultanate of Malacca

The 15th-century sultanate was a great Malay trading power; no authenticated period banner survives, so its red-and-white colors live on in the modern Malacca state flag.

1528–1948

Sultanate of Johor

Successor to the Malacca sultanate, Johor was a major Malay power on the peninsula's tip; its colors survive in the modern Malaysian state of Johor.

1882–1946

North Borneo (Chartered Company)

Modern Sabah was ruled for over sixty years by the British North Borneo Chartered Company, one of the last territories administered by a private trading company.
