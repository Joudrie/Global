Flag of Egypt

Egypt is a transcontinental country spanning northeastern Africa and the Sinai Peninsula of Asia, home to one of the world's oldest and most influential civilisations, which flourished for over 3,000 years along the Nile River. The country is home to iconic ancient monuments including the Pyramids of Giza, the only surviving wonder of the ancient world, and the Sphinx. Egypt controls the Suez Canal, one of the world's most strategically vital shipping routes, connecting the Mediterranean and Red Seas.

What the flag of Egypt means

Egypt's flag uses the colours of the 1952 revolution: red for the time before the revolution and the struggle against colonial rule, white for the bloodless revolution itself, and black for the end of oppression. The golden Eagle of Saladin was adopted in 1984; earlier versions used two green stars, and then the Hawk of Quraish.

How to recognise it

Horizontal red-white-black with the Eagle of Saladin in the centre white stripe.

Did you know?

Egypt is home to the only surviving wonder of the ancient world — the Great Pyramid of Giza.

Flag history of Egypt

Every flag that has flown over Egypt, from today back through time.

1984–present

Flag of Egypt

In 1984 the golden hawk was replaced by the gold Eagle of Saladin, Egypt's national emblem, centered in the red-white-black tricolour. The colors signify the struggle against occupation, a bright future, and the end of oppression.

1972–1984

Federation of Arab Republics

For the federation of Egypt, Libya, and Syria, the two stars were replaced by a golden Hawk of Quraish on the white band. Egypt kept this hawk flag even after the federation lapsed.

1958–1972

United Arab Republic

The 1958 union of Egypt and Syria replaced the central eagle with two green stars, one for each member state. Syria left in 1961, but Egypt kept the name and this two-star flag for another decade.

1953–1958

Liberation flag (1952 Revolution)

After the 1952 Free Officers' coup, the red-white-black "Arab Liberation" tricolour with the Eagle of Saladin became the national flag once the monarchy was abolished in 1953.

1922–1953

Kingdom of Egypt

When Britain recognized Egyptian independence in 1922, King Fuad I adopted a green field with a white crescent enclosing three white stars — usually said to represent Egypt, Nubia, and Sudan.

1844–1922

Khedivate of Egypt

Founded by Muhammad Ali Pasha — the Albanian commander regarded as the father of modern Egypt — the ruling dynasty governed a nominally Ottoman but effectively independent Egypt, flying a red flag with white crescents and stars. As the Khedivate it built the Suez Canal, then sank into debt and fell under British occupation in 1882, ruling on as a British puppet until the 1922 kingdom.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Egypt.

909–1171

Fatimid Caliphate

The Shia Ismaili Fatimids adopted green as their standard, and green's lasting association with Islam owes much to this dynasty. They founded Cairo as their capital.

1922–1953

Kingdom of Egypt

Adopted under King Fuad I in 1922; the green field's three stars are commonly said to represent Egypt, Nubia, and Sudan.

1972–1977

Federation of Arab Republics

The red-white-black Arab Liberation tricolor with a golden Hawk of Quraish, flown simultaneously as the national flag of all three members — Egypt, Libya, and Syria.

1844–1867

Ottoman Egypt (Khedivate)

A red field with three white crescents each cradling a star, flown under Muhammad Ali's dynasty while Egypt was an autonomous Ottoman province.

1953–1958

Republic of Egypt (1953–1958)

After the Free Officers abolished the monarchy, Egypt flew a red-white-black 'Liberation' flag with a golden Eagle of Saladin, until the 1958 union with Syria as the United Arab Republic.

1882–1922

Khedivate of Egypt

A red field with a white crescent and three stars, flown while Egypt was nominally an Ottoman khedivate but in practice under British occupation, until it became the Kingdom of Egypt in 1922.

1171–1260

Ayyubid Dynasty

Founded by Saladin, the Kurdish-origin dynasty ruled Egypt and Syria and recaptured Jerusalem from the Crusaders, flying a plain yellow banner — the colour the modern Eagle of Saladin recalls.

1250–1517

Mamluk Sultanate

A state ruled by a self-perpetuating caste of slave-soldiers from Cairo, which halted the Mongols at Ain Jalut and dominated Egypt and the Levant until the Ottoman conquest.
