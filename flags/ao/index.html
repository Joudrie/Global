Flag of Angola

Angola is a large country on the southwestern coast of Africa, rich in oil, diamonds, and other natural resources. It was a Portuguese colony for nearly 500 years before gaining independence in 1975, which was immediately followed by a devastating civil war that lasted until 2002. Luanda, the capital, grew rapidly into one of Africa's most expensive cities due to the oil boom, though the country's wealth remains very unevenly distributed.

What the flag of Angola means

Red stands for the blood shed during the liberation struggle and black for Africa. The yellow emblem, half a cog wheel crossed by a machete with a star, echoes the hammer and sickle: the wheel for industrial workers, the machete for farm workers and the armed struggle, and the star for international solidarity and progress. The flag is based on that of the MPLA, which took power at independence in 1975.

How to recognise it

Horizontal red-over-black with a yellow machete, star, and half-gear wheel in the centre.

Did you know?

Angola has one of the fastest-growing economies in Africa, fuelled by vast oil reserves.

Flag history of Angola

Every flag that has flown over Angola, from today back through time.

1975–present

Flag of Angola

Adopted at independence from Portugal in 1975, red over black with a yellow machete, half-cogwheel, and star from the ruling MPLA's emblem. The design deliberately echoes the Soviet hammer-and-sickle.

1655–1975

Portuguese Angola

Angola was a Portuguese possession for some four centuries — first a source of enslaved people for Brazil, later a settler colony — flying the flag of Portugal. Portugal's refusal to decolonise triggered a 14-year war of independence from 1961; when the 1974 Carnation Revolution toppled the regime in Lisbon, Angola finally won independence in 1975, only to fall straight into a long civil war.

1390–1857

Kingdom of Kongo

Long before the Portuguese, northern Angola and the lower Congo were the heart of the Kingdom of Kongo, a sophisticated Central African state whose king converted to Christianity in 1491 and corresponded with European monarchs as an equal. The Atlantic slave trade slowly hollowed it out, and it survived as a shadow until the Portuguese abolished the monarchy in the 19th century.

1975

Republic of Cabinda

The separatist republic declared in the oil-rich Cabinda enclave when Portugal withdrew, before Angola annexed it.

1600–1885

Kingdom of Kakongo

A small Kongo kingdom of the Cabinda coast, north of the Congo River mouth.

1600–1900

Chokwe Kingdom

The states of the Chokwe people of northeastern Angola, celebrated for their sophisticated art and ivory carving.

1515–1671

Kingdom of Ndongo

An Mbundu kingdom of Angola whose Queen Njinga waged a decades-long war against Portuguese slavers.

1500–1900

Mbunda Kingdom

A kingdom of the Mbunda people in southeastern Angola, known for its woodcarving and the Makishi masquerade.

1975–present

Republic of Cabinda

An oil-rich exclave separated from the rest of Angola by a strip of DR Congo; its separatist movement FLEC has flown this blue-yellow-red flag with a star in a long, low-level independence struggle.
