Flag of Brazil

Brazil is the largest country in South America and the fifth-largest in the world, home to the Amazon Rainforest — the world's largest tropical rainforest — which contains roughly 10% of all species on Earth. Brazil has one of the world's most diverse populations, shaped by Indigenous, African, Portuguese, and subsequent immigrant heritage from around the world. It is renowned for its Carnival festival, passionate football culture, and the music genres of samba and bossa nova.

What the flag of Brazil means

Green and yellow come from the royal houses of Emperor Pedro I and Empress Maria Leopoldina, though they are popularly read as forests and gold. The blue globe shows the night sky over Rio de Janeiro on 15 November 1889, when the republic was proclaimed, with 27 stars for the states and the Federal District, and the positivist motto “Ordem e Progresso”, order and progress.

How to recognise it

Green field with a yellow diamond, blue globe with stars and the motto "Ordem e Progresso."

Did you know?

Brazil is the only country in the Americas where Portuguese is the official language.

Flag history of Brazil

Every flag that has flown over Brazil, from today back through time.

1992–present

Flag of Brazil (27 stars)

Updated in 1992 to 27 stars after several territories became states, each star representing a state or the Federal District. It is one of the few national flags that maps an actual astronomical sky.

1968–1992

Republic flag (1968)

The constellation was rearranged and a star added for the new state of Acre. The layout still encoded the night sky over Rio de Janeiro on the morning the republic was proclaimed.

1889–1960

Republic flag (21 stars)

The classic green-and-yellow design with a blue celestial globe, the motto "Ordem e Progresso," and 21 stars. The sky depicts the constellations as seen over Rio on the morning of 15 November 1889.

1889

Provisional Republic flag

A short-lived flag inspired by the United States, with green-and-yellow stripes and a blue canton, flown for only days after the monarchy fell before the starry design replaced it.

1822–1889

Empire of Brazil

A green field with a yellow rhombus framing the imperial arms. Green stood for the House of Braganza and yellow for the Habsburgs — the houses of Emperor Pedro I and Empress Maria Leopoldina.

Separatist republic of the era:

1836–1845

Riograndense Republic (Piratini)

The "Ragamuffin War" saw Brazil's southernmost province secede as the Riograndense Republic from 1836 to 1845 — the longest civil war in Brazilian history. Giuseppe Garibaldi fought on the rebel side before his later Italian campaigns.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Brazil.

1822–1889

Empire of Brazil

A green field with a yellow rhombus bearing the imperial arms ringed by 20 stars. The republican coup of 1889 swapped the crowned arms for the celestial globe and 'Ordem e Progresso'.

1893–1895

Principality of Trinidad

A yellow field with a red cross, designed by adventurer James Harden-Hickey for his unrecognized micronation on the uninhabited Brazilian island of Trindade.

1887–1904

Republic of Counani

A self-proclaimed republic in the disputed France–Brazil border zone of Guiana, promoted by French adventurers but never internationally recognized.

1630–1654

Dutch Brazil (New Holland)

A Dutch tricolor with the Dutch West India Company monogram, flown over the sugar colony the Dutch held in northeastern Brazil for a quarter-century.

1836–1845

Riograndense Republic

The breakaway republic of the decade-long Farroupilha (Ragamuffin) War in southern Brazil — three diagonal stripes of green, red, and yellow.

1839

Republic of Juliana

A short-lived republic proclaimed at Laguna in Santa Catarina during the Ragamuffin War, allied with the Riograndense rebels. The Italian revolutionary Giuseppe Garibaldi fought for it.

1824

Confederation of the Equator

A short-lived secessionist confederation of northeastern Brazilian provinces revolting against the new emperor; a sky-blue field with a shield reading 'Confederação'.

1899–1903

Republic of Acre

Rubber-tappers in the Bolivia–Brazil borderland declared an independent republic over the lucrative Acre territory; its diagonal yellow-green flag survives as the Brazilian state flag.

1821–1828

Cisplatine Province

The name given to present-day Uruguay while it was annexed first to Portugal and then to the Empire of Brazil, until the 1828 war secured its independence.
