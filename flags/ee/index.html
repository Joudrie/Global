Flag of Estonia

Estonia is one of the three Baltic states on the eastern shore of the Baltic Sea, having re-declared independence from the Soviet Union in 1991 as one of its first republics to do so. It rapidly transformed into one of the world's most digitally advanced societies, pioneering e-government, digital voting, and the concept of e-residency. Tallinn, the capital, has one of the best-preserved medieval old towns in all of Northern Europe.

What the flag of Estonia means

Blue, black and white were first the colours of the Estonian Students' Society, whose flag was consecrated in Otepää in 1884. Blue is often read as the sky, loyalty and hope; black as the dark soil and the hardships of the past; and white as snowy winters and the striving for freedom. Banned under Soviet rule, the flag was raised again on Tall Hermann tower in Tallinn on 24 February 1989.

How to recognise it

Horizontal blue-black-white — the only flag in the world with this colour combination.

Did you know?

Estonia was the first country in the world to use internet voting in national elections, in 2005.

Flag history of Estonia

Every flag that has flown over Estonia, from today back through time.

1990–present

Flag of Estonia (restored)

The blue-black-white tricolour, first raised by Estonian students in 1884, was restored in 1990 as the USSR collapsed. Blue is the sky, black the soil and hardship, white the people's striving for freedom.

1953–1990

Estonian SSR

The Soviet-era flag placed a gold hammer, sickle, and star on red above blue wavy bands evoking the Baltic Sea.

1940–1953

Estonian SSR (early)

After Soviet annexation in 1940, a plain red flag with gold "ENSV" lettering and a hammer and sickle replaced the tricolour until the 1953 redesign.

1918–1940

Independence era

The same blue-black-white tricolour became the national flag at independence in 1918, before it was banned under Soviet rule and later restored.

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Estonia.

1922–1991

Soviet Union

The gold hammer and sickle beneath a red star stood for the worker-peasant alliance. The exact shade and proportions were fixed by Soviet law in 1955.

1953–1990

Estonian SSR

A red flag with a wavy blue-and-white Baltic band at the base, the Soviet flag of Estonia, replaced when the blue-black-white national tricolor was restored in 1990.
