Tunisia is the northernmost country in Africa, where the continent reaches closest to Europe with just 140 kilometres separating it from the Italian island of Sicily. The ancient Carthaginian civilisation, a rival of Rome, was centred near modern-day Tunis. Tunisia was the birthplace of the Arab Spring in late 2010, when protests sparked a wave of revolutionary movements that spread across the Arab world.

What the flag of Tunisia means

Tunisia's flag dates from the 1830s, under the Husainid beys, and closely follows the Ottoman flag. The red crescent and star inside a white disc are symbols of Islam and of unity, and red is said to represent the blood of the martyrs. It became the flag of independent Tunisia in 1956 and its exact design was fixed by law in 1999.

More about the flag

The crescent and star echo the Ottoman flag because Tunisia was nominally part of the Ottoman Empire when the beys adopted it, though it had become largely self-governing. After 75 years as a French protectorate, Tunisia became independent in 1956 and a republic in 1957. The 2011 Tunisian Revolution, which began the Arab Spring, was fought under the same flag.

Flag history of Tunisia

Every flag that has flown over Tunisia, from today back through time.

1999–present

Flag of Tunisia

A 1999 law formalized the flag for the first time, only slightly adjusting the crescent and proportions of a design that had flown almost unchanged since 1831 — among the oldest in the Arab world.

1831–1999

Husainid red flag

Hussein II created this red flag with a white disc, crescent, and star around 1831 to distinguish the Tunisian fleet. It survived the French protectorate and independence essentially unchanged.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Tunisia.

1705–1881

Beylik of Tunis

The Husainid beys ruled Tunis as an autonomous Ottoman province, flying an early ensign with horizontal bands and a crescent, before the French protectorate of 1881.

1229–1574

Hafsid Dynasty

The Berber dynasty that ruled Ifriqiya (modern Tunisia) from Tunis for over three centuries, a major Mediterranean trading power, until it fell to the Ottomans.
