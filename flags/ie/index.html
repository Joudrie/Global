Flag of Ireland

Ireland is an island nation in northwestern Europe known for its lush green landscapes, rich literary heritage from writers like Oscar Wilde and James Joyce, and vibrant Celtic culture. The island is divided between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, which is part of the United Kingdom. Ireland joined the European Economic Community in 1973 and has since become one of Europe's most prosperous economies, home to the European headquarters of many global technology companies.

What the flag of Ireland means

Green represents the Gaelic and Catholic tradition, orange the Protestant supporters of William of Orange, and the white between them a lasting peace between the two. The tricolour was presented by Thomas Francis Meagher in 1848, inspired by the flags of revolutionary France, and was widely flown after the 1916 Easter Rising. The 1937 constitution confirmed it as the national flag.

How to recognise it

Vertical green-white-orange. Côte d'Ivoire is the exact mirror: orange-white-green.

Did you know?

Ireland is one of the few countries where Halloween originated — from the Celtic festival of Samhain.

Flag history of Ireland

Every flag that has flown over Ireland, from today back through time.

1922–present

Green-white-orange tricolour

First flown by Thomas Francis Meagher in 1848 and raised over the GPO during the 1916 Rising, the tricolour was used by the Irish Free State from 1922 and given constitutional status in 1937. Green represents Catholics, orange Protestants, and the white the hoped-for peace between them.

1642–1916

Green flag with gold harp

A green field bearing a golden harp was recognized as the flag of Ireland from the mid-1600s and remained the dominant nationalist emblem right up to the 1916 Easter Rising. The same design survives today as the flag of Leinster.

1783–present

St Patrick's Saltire

A red X-shaped cross on white, adopted by the Order of Saint Patrick in 1783 and folded into the Union Flag in 1801. Its status as a genuinely Irish symbol is disputed — it was largely an Anglo-Irish heraldic device.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Ireland.

1916

Irish Republic (1916)

The green flag with gold lettering 'Irish Republic' flown over Dublin's General Post Office during the Easter Rising. The original captured banner survives in Dublin.

1642–1652

Confederate Ireland

The Catholic Confederation based at Kilkenny flew a green field with a gold harp and a white canton bearing a red cross during the Eleven Years' War.

1171–1541

Lordship of Ireland

Three gold crowns on light blue with a white border, the banner of the medieval English lordship before the gold-harp arms replaced it under Henry VIII.

1542–1801

Kingdom of Ireland

The red saltire of St Patrick on white represented the kingdom under the English (later British) crown; it survives as the Irish element of the modern Union Flag.
