Flag of Pakistan

Pakistan is a South Asian country created from British India in 1947 as a homeland for South Asian Muslims during Partition. It is the world's second-most populous Muslim-majority country and has a complex ethnic tapestry including Punjabis, Pashtuns, Sindhis, and Baloch peoples. Pakistan is home to K2 (the world's second-highest mountain), the ancient Indus Valley city of Mohenjo-daro, and some of the most spectacular mountain scenery on Earth in the Karakoram and Hindu Kush ranges.

What the flag of Pakistan means

Dark green represents the Muslim majority and the white stripe the country's religious minorities. The crescent stands for progress and the five-pointed star for light and knowledge. Based on the flag of the All-India Muslim League, it was adopted on 11 August 1947, three days before independence.

How to recognise it

Dark green with a white vertical stripe at the hoist and a white crescent and star.

Did you know?

Pakistan is home to five of the world's fourteen 8,000-metre peaks.

Flag history of Pakistan

Every flag that has flown over Pakistan, from today back through time.

1947–present

Flag of Pakistan

Adopted days before independence in 1947. The white hoist stripe represents religious minorities, while the green field and white crescent-and-star represent the Muslim majority. It has been stable ever since.

1858–1947

British India (the Raj)

Before Partition, the lands that became Pakistan were part of British India, governed under the Union Jack and the unofficial "Star of India" red ensign. The Muslim League's campaign for a separate Muslim homeland culminated in the 1947 Partition, which created Pakistan as two wings (West and East) flanking India — the basis of its green-and-white flag was the League's own banner.

1666–1955

Khanate of Kalat

A Baloch khanate in what is now southwestern Pakistan, briefly independent in 1947–48 before acceding to Pakistan.

1783–1843

Talpur Dynasty

The Baloch dynasty that ruled Sindh (in modern Pakistan), flying the flag of Sindh, until the British conquered it at the Battle of Miani.

Related historical states

Empires, kingdoms and republics that once covered part of Pakistan.

1802–1955

Princely State of Bahawalpur

A wealthy princely state in the Punjab desert ruled by the Abbasi Nawabs, whose ruler was among the first to accede to the new nation of Pakistan in 1947.

1526–1857

Mughal Empire

One of history's great empires, ruling most of the Indian subcontinent at its height; its green 'Alam' banner bore a lion and rising sun. Builders of the Taj Mahal, the Mughals were finally deposed by the British after the 1857 rebellion.
