What's new

Globalio gets new games, flags and fixes every month. Here's what changed recently.

A tidier Play tab and Settings

28 November 2026

  • Settings is easier to read: short sections for Account, Your progress, Help and About. Patch notes now open with a button to contact support or request a feature.
  • Play is shorter: an All button beside the search box (the row of category buttons is gone), then the Trending carousel, your Favourites, Creator's Picks, a big View all games button, then the categories. Jump back in, Recommended for you, Top games and Discover something new are gone (Surprise me still picks a random game).
  • On the All page, thumbnails fit their squares: Dead or Alive shows the Soviet hammer and sickle instead of a red block, Stat Clash shows the Vatican's emblem, Lineage and Flag Timeline stack their flags, and Spoken Here and Guess the Language no longer get cut off.
  • The Daily Rituals shelf is split up: Connections is in Spot It, Fun Fact and World Cup 2026 in Learn the World, and Flag Bracket and Tier List Maker in One Glance.

Fixes from a check of every game

28 November 2026

  • Type-in games accept a country's code on Enter (BH for Bahrain), as the box says.
  • Build the Flag: the ticks and crosses on the result are easy to see on any colour, and stripe names sit inside their stripes.
  • Symbol Hunt: after Submit, found, missed and wrong flags are clearly marked, with a Found X of Y line.
  • Lookalikes shows its tip once, under the flags, without pushing them down. GeoPaint and Flag Outline show the real flag without black bars, and Flag Outline says "1 guess left".
  • The Crop's zoom bar ends on the last step, and Flag Identifier's counts have thousands commas.
  • Historical Flags and Identity Flags scroll the note and Next button into view after you answer. Silhouette's guess box no longer zooms the page on iPhones.
  • Subdivision Stumper's "Other way to play" switches to the other mode, and Province Roulette's banner shows the flag on wide screens instead of an empty white box.
  • Lineage shows each country's flags oldest to newest, ending on today's flag (Turkey and South Korea were out of order), and skips missing images.
  • Challenge Mode and Higher / Lower no longer move the answer buttons between questions, Capital Match no longer jumps on the first mistake, and Odd Border Out marks the right answer green and a wrong pick red.
  • Describe-It's last tip no longer names the country, Flag Timeline's ticks show on every flag, Atlas keeps its zoom button on screen, and Guess the Language says Japanese uses kanji and kana.
  • Capital Cities picks wrong answers from the same region, as intended. US City Flags never shows two "Springfield" buttons, its note no longer pushes the answers down, and Yonkers' flag loads.
  • Stat Clash and Pride Roulette no longer jump when you answer, Flag Forgery's side labels fit on phones, and Border Path names the countries on its shortest route.
  • Text boxes no longer zoom the page in on iPhones. Dallas, Oklahoma City and Jersey City were showing a county or state flag instead of their own, so they're left out until their real flags can be added.
  • Codex: tapping a region lower down a long list (Thuringia, Cardiff) now scrolls its details into view, and long region names break more cleanly on phones.
  • World Cup: a nation's Codex entry keeps its Back button on screen, and the phone's Back gesture now closes Play's All page instead of leaving the site.

Creator's Picks, a View all page, tidier patch notes and Codex fixes

28 November 2026

  • Play has a new Creator's Picks row: my personal favourites, in the order I'd play them (Abroad, Flag Fusion, Flag Studio, Flag Identifier, Guess the Language, Real or Bot, Lineage).
  • Play: Top games now comes before Recommended for you, Jump back in no longer repeats games you've starred, and the All chip (or View all at the bottom) opens every game A–Z in one block, four to a row.
  • The Codex no longer shows stray code after some names (Eastern Andalusians and the two Mirandese proposals), and flags whose image was missing are back: the Commonwealth of Nations, the Baltic Assembly, Montgomery (Alabama), and Iraq's Basra, Babylon and Nineveh governorates. Flags with no image left anywhere are hidden instead of showing an empty box.
  • Flag Fusion: a fused flag that wouldn't draw on some iPhones (a small question-mark icon) now draws properly.
  • Patch notes now start closed: tap a day to see its list of updates, then tap an update to read what changed.
  • The button at the bottom of the Codex is now Mega Codex: it opens the Mega Codex with every flag, sorted A–Z, Z–A or shuffled, and you can tap any flag for its description.

Flag Identifier finds city flags

27 November 2026

  • Flag Identifier now searches 3,619 flags, up from 3,230: 389 city flags of Europe joined, so describing red-black-red with three white crosses finds Amsterdam. Pick City as the kind to search city flags first.
  • Brest in France is back in the Mega Codex, beside Brest in Belarus.

City flags in the Codex (beta)

27 November 2026

  • A new City Flags section lists cities by continent, then by country: open France and you'll find its 24 cities from Paris down, each with its flag, population and, for well-known flags, a line on what it shows.
  • Europe comes first, with 491 cities across 44 countries. The 124 American city flags now sit under North America → United States, sorted by size.
  • All of them are in the Mega Codex, which now holds over 5,000 flags, and in a new City Flags deck in Flashcards.

  • The Belarusian People's Republic (1918) shows its white-red-white flag instead of today's Belarus flag.

New: regions of about 80 more countries (Kenya's counties, Thailand's provinces, Colombia, Nigeria, Ukraine, Poland and more), nearly 800 historical flags, about 600 peoples' flags, overseas territories like Gibraltar and the Falklands, and more organisations.

New game: Spoken Here

27 November 2026

  • Each round is a pie chart of one country's languages from its latest census or survey, with the year it's from (“Mother tongue, 2021 census”, and “From 2001, 25 years ago” when the figures are old). Name the country from four choices, or type it for double points.
  • Easy and medium name the languages; hard shows only their families, from Romance and Bantu to Turkic and Austronesian. After each answer you see the flag, every language counted and where the figures come from. 100 countries.

Flag Identifier now finds 1,192 flags, historical ones included

Flag DNA now plays like Wordle

27 November 2026

  • Each guess lists only what its own flag has: colours, pattern and symbols. Green means the mystery flag has it too, yellow means its stripes or cross run the other way, grey means it doesn't. Nothing is said about things your guess doesn't have.
  • A Known so far panel collects what you've confirmed and ruled out, and shows how many colours the mystery flag has. Six guesses, and your best streak is saved.

Spot the Error uses real forgeries, and Flag Families stays still

27 November 2026

  • Spot the Error: each flag now has one doctored detail, like a missing star, a mirrored crescent, an off-centre disc or a wrong-coloured band. Tap where it is, then see the real flag beside it with the mistake circled. It gets harder as your streak grows, and your best streak is saved.
  • Flag Forgery has nine new fakes, among them China, Switzerland, India, Japan and Canada.
  • Flag Families: flags no longer shift when you pick one up or sort it.

“Did you mean…?”, a fairer Build the Flag and harder Frankenflag cuts

New: Flag Identifier

24 November 2026

  • Saw a flag on the drive or on TV and can't name it? Describe it and Globalio finds it. Pick its layout (stripes, a cross, a triangle at the pole, a circle in the middle…), the colours you remember, then anything on it, like stars, a sun, a crescent, a coat of arms or writing.
  • Every country flag is ranked by how well it fits, so one colour remembered wrong doesn't hide the answer: that flag shows as close instead. The count of flags that fit is always on screen, and you can open the list or change an answer at any step.
  • Tap a match to see the flag up close, how to tell it apart, and a link to its meaning and history.
  • A new page, globalio.app/flag-identifier/, lists every flag by design: Nordic crosses, triangles at the hoist, suns, crescents, red, white and blue flags and more.

New game: Flag Fusion

17 November 2026

  • One flag, fused from three: a made-up flag takes its structure from one country, its emblem from a second and its colours from a third. Name all three.
  • Brazil's diamond with Afghanistan's emblem, painted in Bhutan's yellow, orange and white, is one. A box you name turns green and stays; five wrong guesses are shared between the three boxes.
  • Five flags a game on easy (famous flags), medium or hard (obscure ones), with a best score for each. After every flag you see the three real flags it was made from.
  • When countries share a layout (the Nordic crosses, Czechia and the Philippines) or a set of colours (Germany and Belgium), every one of them counts. 91 layouts, 33 emblems and the colours of 41 countries.

Two new games: Countries Abroad and Native Names

14 November 2026

  • Countries Abroad: name the country from its name in another language. Allemagne, Niemcy, Saksa and Almanya are all Germany, Ruotsi is Sweden and Viro is Estonia. 1,900 names for 184 countries in 57 languages.
  • Native Names: name the country from what it calls itself. Suomi is Finland, Magyarország is Hungary, Sakartvelo is Georgia and Druk Yul is Bhutan. 85 names for 68 countries in 64 languages, each in its own script with a Latin spelling under it.
  • Both play like Cities Abroad: ten right before three strikes on easy, medium or hard. Typing scores double, and a hint (the region and the first letter) halves the points.
  • Names spelled like the English one are left out, a small slip in spelling still counts, and look-alike countries never count for each other: Niger is not Nigeria and Austria is not Australia.

New game: Cities Abroad

14 November 2026

  • Name the city from its name in another language: Wien is Vienna, Benetke is Venice, Kaupmannahöfn is Copenhagen and Pietari is Saint Petersburg. Get ten right before three strikes.
  • Capitals and famous cities alike, from New York and Istanbul to Cape Town, Kraków and Thessaloniki. Names spelled like the English one are left out, so every round makes you think.
  • Easy keeps to sound-alikes such as Moskau and Londres, Medium strays further (Varsovia, Monaco di Baviera) and Hard brings Bécs, iKapa and the Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, Armenian, Korean and Japanese scripts, with a Latin spelling under each.
  • The stranger the name, the more it scores. Typing doubles the points, a hint halves them, and a small slip in spelling still counts.
  • 1,732 names for 310 cities in 57 languages, from Wikidata, each checked against that language's Wikipedia. The end of a game lists every name you saw.

New game: Atlas

26 October 2026

  • Explore a real world map printed between 1784 and 2015, read its borders and place names, then guess the year it was made. Printed dates are masked.
  • The map fills the screen with the guess right under it: slide to a year or fine-tune with −10, −1, +1 and +10, then see your guess and the answer side by side on a timeline.
  • One daily map for everyone, changing at midnight UK time, plus an archive of 197 maps from the Library of Congress and the David Rumsey Map Collection to practise on.
  • Zoom in and the original scan sharpens tile by tile. After your guess, the clues explain which names and borders give the date away, with a link to the original.

Fairer games, everywhere

8 October 2026

  • Two Truths never calls a true statement the lie (Turkey really is in Asia), and tells you when you picked a truth.
  • Typing a country works with other names and without accents in every game: USA, UK, Ivory Coast, Holland, Burma, Czechia, Sao Tome. "UK" is never Ukraine, and Enter never guesses for you.
  • A quick double tap can no longer skip a question, cast two votes in Flag Bracket, or jump past a result.

  • The suggestion lists in Silhouette, The Crop, The Peel and Composer show names only, so they no longer give the answer away.
  • Your progress in long Flag Sets is saved as you go, the daily picks up where you left off, and the streak shows 0 after a missed day.
  • The phone's Back button takes you home instead of leaving the site.
  • Change your mind mid-move: in Flag Timeline, tap a placed flag to take it back and put another in its place; in Build the Flag, tap a piece again to put it down or tap a placed piece to take it off; in Spot the Error, tap a band again to unpick it. Flag Bracket can undo your last pick (even the final), The Peel lets you go back to scratching, Capital Match works capital-first too, Describe It no longer charges a clue for a repeated guess, and Pride Roulette asks before 10/All restarts a run.
  • The Codex keeps your search and place when you switch tabs, opens Flag of the Day right on its entry, and finds countries by any name.

  • Flag Families never has two right ways to sort, and Symbol Hunt no longer marks a right tap wrong.
  • Corrected tips and facts for Turkey, Cyprus, Liechtenstein, Mozambique, Kazakhstan, Croatia, San Marino, Chile, Lebanon and more. Malay has its own sentence in Language Quiz, and Crimea is listed once.
